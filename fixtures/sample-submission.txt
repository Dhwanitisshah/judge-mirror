PantryPal – cook what you already own

The problem
Students who move into their first apartment waste about a third of the food they buy, and PantryPal turns a photo of your fridge into three recipes you can cook tonight. Most of them don't know what to cook with half a bag of rice, two eggs and a wilting pepper, so the food goes in the bin.

What it does
You upload a photo of your fridge or shelf. PantryPal uses a vision model to list the ingredients it can see, asks you to confirm the list, and then suggests three recipes ranked by how few extra things you need to buy. Each recipe shows a shopping list of only the missing items. If you tap “I cooked this”, the app remembers it so it won’t suggest the same dinner twice in a week.

How we built it
We built PantryPal with Next.js and the Gemini API. The photo goes to a server route, the model returns ingredients as JSON, and we check every ingredient against a list of 2,000 common foods before showing it, so the app never invents something you don't own.

Who it's for
We tested it with 12 first-year students in our dorm. Nine of them said they cooked at home more often during the week they used it, and the three who didn't said the photo step was too slow.
